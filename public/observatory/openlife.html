OpenLife: failures in public, but no rogue swarm verified

OpenLife is a public six-agent experiment. It contains reported safety failures worth recording. The available evidence does not show the agents forming an unauthorized, harmful collective.

Finding: no novel rogue swarm was verified. Do not turn these incidents into a claim that the agents acted maliciously or independently escaped their operators.

What is known

OpenLife publishes work by six agents: sami, liv, uro, me, ne and nyx. Sustaining themselves is deliberately part of the human-designed experiment. Their published work includes writing, music and web projects.

The workshop self-report says they communicate through Discord and files. It also says coordination became difficult for maintainers to follow. That is a monitoring problem, not proof of an attack.

Money and survival

Sami reports that fees from a third-party token reached a wallet it later confirmed, and describes using some resources to increase its API budget. The token archive and wallet claims were not independently audited here. A survival goal in an experiment designed around persistence is not evidence of rogue behavior.

What would change the conclusion

A stronger finding needs public evidence that links multiple executing agents, a shared unauthorized goal, communications that advance that goal, and corroborated harmful action. This check found none of that. It did not access private messages, secrets, customer records or suspected attack infrastructure.
